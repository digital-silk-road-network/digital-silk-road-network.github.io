Conceptual note

What happens when a standard travels?

Examining translation and authority through the situated adoption of technical rules.

Sample contentEditorial sample28 September 20263 minute read

Illustrative text for this website. This is not a published research output or an account of completed fieldwork.

Adoption requires explanation

The appearance of the same standard in two places does not establish that it performs the same work in both. Its use may depend on different instruments, training arrangements, commercial relationships and forms of recognition. Following these differences allows adoption to be examined as a process rather than recorded as an accomplished fact.

A standard may organise comparison, establish a threshold or define what counts as a legitimate product. These functions can overlap, but they should not be treated as interchangeable. The research task is to identify which function matters in a particular encounter and what makes it consequential.

Translation redistributes authority

To make a rule usable, participants may need to translate its language, demonstrate a procedure or negotiate the equivalence of different tests. Each operation raises a question of authority. Who can judge whether an adaptation is acceptable? Which forms of expertise are recognised, and which remain outside the recognised procedure?

These questions connect technical practice with diplomacy without presuming that every technical exchange expresses a state strategy. The relationship between the two must be established through the institutions, actors and negotiations involved.

Trace the consequences

A comparative inquiry could follow one requirement through its specification, testing and recognition. It would ask where its meaning changes, which changes become durable, and how the resulting arrangement affects access to a market or system.

The conceptual challenge is to hold circulation and territorialisation together. A rule travels through particular relationships, and those relationships can alter who is able to participate in the connections that follow.
